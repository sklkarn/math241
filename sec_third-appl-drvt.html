    Subsection 3.6.1 Measurement of Rate

    Example 3.6.1.

    If the sides of a square decreasing at the rate of \(1.5 \, cm/s\text{.}\) Find the rate of decrease of its perimeter.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given: \(\frac{dx}{dt} = -1.5\, cm/s\) \(\frac{dp}{dt} = ?\text{.}\)
    \begin{equation*} p=4x \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, \frac{dp}{dt} = 4\frac{dx}{dt} = - 6 \, cm/s. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.2.

    The median of an equilateral triangle is increasing at the rate of \(2\sqrt{3} \, cm/s.\) Find the rate at which its side is increasing.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given: \(\frac{dy}{dt} = 2\sqrt{3} \, cm/s\) \(\frac{dx}{dt} = ?\text{.}\)
    \begin{align*} \sin 60^0 \amp = \frac{y}{x}\\ y \amp =x\sin 60^o = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} x\\ or, \, \frac{dy}{dt} \amp = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \frac{dx}{dt} \\ or, \,2\sqrt{3} \amp = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \frac{dx}{dt} \\ or, \frac{dx}{dt} \amp = 4\, cm/s \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.3.

    A spherical baloon is expanding. If the radius is increasing at the rate of \(5\) inch per minute. Find the rate at which the volume increases (in cubic inches per minute) when the radius is 10 inch.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} V= \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \end{equation*}
    Differentiate w.r.t. "x", we get -
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{4}{3}\pi (3r^2)\frac{dr}{dt} = 4\pi r^2\times 5 = 20 \pi r^2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, \frac{dr}{dt}\bigg|_{r=10} = 20 \pi \times 10^2 = 2000\pi. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.4.

    If the radius of a circle is decreasing at uniform rate of \(2 \, cm/s\text{,}\) then find the rate of change of area the circle when \(r=20 \, cm\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given \(\frac{dr}{dt}=-2 \, cm/s\text{.}\) For a circle
    \begin{equation*} A= \pi r^2 \end{equation*}
    Differentiate w.r.t. "x", we get -
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r \frac{dr}{dt} = 2\pi r (-2) = -4\pi r \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, \frac{dA}{dt}\bigg|_{r=20} = -4 \pi \times 20 = -80\pi \, cm^2/s. \end{equation*}
    i.e., the rate of area of the circle is decreasing by \(80 \pi\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.5.

    The tangent of an angle increases four times as the angle itself. At what rate the sine of the angle increaseses w.r.t. the angle?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given
    \begin{equation*} \frac{d}{dt}\tan\theta =4\frac{d\theta}{dt} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, \frac{d}{d\theta}\tan\theta =4 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, \sec^2 \theta =4 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, \cos^2 \theta =\frac{1}{4} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} i.e., \, \cos \theta =\pm \frac{1}{2} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Hence,
    \begin{equation*} \frac{d}{d\theta}\sin\theta = \cos \theta = + \frac{1}{2} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.6.

    A \(13\, m \) ladder initially leaning against the vertical wall starts slidding on the floor at the rate of \(2\, cm/s.\) How fast is the height of ladder on the wall is decreasing when the foot of the ladder is \(5\, m\) away form the wall?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given: \(\frac{dx}{dt}=2\, cm/s\text{,}\) \(l=13\, m\text{,}\) \(\frac{dy}{dt}\bigg|_x=5\, m\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation} y^2 = 13^2-x^2\tag{3.6.1} \end{equation}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0-2x\frac{dx}{dt} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, \frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{x}{y}\frac{dx}{dt} \end{equation*}
    Now from (3.6.1), when \(x=5\, m\text{,}\) we get - \(y=\sqrt{13^2-5^2} = 12 \, m\)
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \,\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{5}{12}\times 2 = -\frac{5}{6} \, cm/s. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.7.

    A particle moves along a curve \(6y=x^3+2,\) find the points on the curve at which \(y\) coordinate is changing 8 times as fast as \(x\) coordinate.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given:
    \begin{equation} \frac{dy}{dx} =8\tag{3.6.2} \end{equation}
    \begin{equation*} 6y=x^3+2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 6\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation} or, \, \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x^2}{2}\tag{3.6.3} \end{equation}
    Therefore,
    \begin{equation*} \frac{x^2}{2} = 8 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, x^2 = 16 \, \Rightarrow \, x = \pm 4 \end{equation*}
    Now,
    \begin{equation*} 6y_1=4^3+2 \, \Rightarrow \, y_1 = 11 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 6y_2=(-4)^3+2 \, \Rightarrow \, y_2 = -\frac{31}{3} \end{equation*}
    Hence the points are \((4,11),(-4,--\frac{31}{3})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.8.

    Water is dripping out of a conical funnel at a uniform rate of \(4 \, cc/s\) through a tiny hole at the vertex in bottom, when the slant height of water is \(3 \, cm\text{,}\) find rate of decrease of slant height, it is given that the vertical angle is \(120^o\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dV}{dt} = -4 cc/s. \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dl}{dt}\bigg|_{l=3cm} = ? \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2h \end{equation*}
    From figure,
    \begin{equation*} \cos \frac{\theta}{2} = \frac{h}{l} \end{equation*}
    here \(l\) is slant height of water.
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, \cos (60) =\frac{h}{l} \, \Rightarrow h = l\cos (60) = \frac{l}{2} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} and \, \sin (60) =\frac{r}{l} \, \Rightarrow r = l\sin (60) = l\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} hence, \, V = \frac{1}{3}\pi (l\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})^2(frac{l}{2}) = \frac{1}{8}\pi l^3 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} now, \, \frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{1}{8}3\pi l^2\frac{dl}{dt} = \frac{3}{8}\pi l^2\frac{dl}{dt} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, \frac{dV}{dt}\bigg|_{l=3} = \frac{3}{8}\pi 3^2\frac{dl}{dt}\bigg|_{l=3} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} o, \, -4 = \frac{3}{8}\pi 3^2\frac{dl}{dt}\bigg|_{l=3} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, \frac{dl}{dt}\bigg|_{l=3} = \frac{-4\times 8}{3\times 3^2\times \pi} = -\frac{32}{27 \pi}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.9.

    A light source is at center of circle, the circumference of which a horse is runing with speed of \(20 \, km/h\text{.}\) A fence is along the tangent to the circle at the point from where horse starts running. Find the speed with which the shadow of horse move along the fence at the moment when it covers \(\frac{1}{8}^{th} \) of the circle.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{ds}{dt} =20 km/h, \, \frac{dy}{dt}\bigg|_{s=\frac{1}{8}c} =? \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \tan\theta =\frac{y}{r} \, \Rightarrow \, y =r \tan\theta \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dy}{dt} = r\sec^2\theta \frac{d\theta}{dt}+\tan\theta \cancelto{0}{\frac{dr}{dt}} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} s= r\theta \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{ds}{dt} = r\frac{d\theta}{dt} +\theta\cancelto{0}{\frac{dr}{dt} } \end{equation*}
    🔗
    do your self.

    Example 3.6.10.

    A man \(2 \,m\) high walks at a uniform speed of \(6 /, m/min,\) away from lamppost, \(5 \,m\) high. Find rate at which length of its shadow increases.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dx}{dt} = 6 \, m/min, \, \frac{dl}{dt} = ? \end{equation*}
    🔗
    From similar triangles
    \begin{equation*} \frac{5}{2}= \frac{x+l}{l} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{x}{l} = \frac{5}{2}-1 = \frac{3}{2} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} x= \frac{3}{2} l \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{3}{2} \frac{dl}{dt} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \cancelto{2}{6} = \frac{\cancel{3}}{2} \frac{dl}{dt} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dl}{dt} = 4 \, m/min. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.11.

    A variable triangle is inscribed in a circle of radius \(r\) when rate of change of particular side is \(r\) times the rate of change of opposite angle, then find that angle at that instant.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Given:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{da}{dt} =r\frac{dA}{dt}, \, A=? \end{equation*}
    🔗
    From sine formula
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c} = \frac{1}{2r} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} a=2r\sin A \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{da}{dt} = 2r\cos A\frac{dA}{dt} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \cancel{r\frac{dA}{dt}} = 2\cancel{r}\cos A\cancel{\frac{dA}{dt}} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 2\cos A =1 \, \Rightarrow \, A = 60^o. \end{equation*}
    Note:
    🔗
    The inscribed angles subtended by the same arc are equal. That is, \(\angle BAC = \angle BDC.\) Now area of \(\triangle ABC = \frac{1}{2} \,(base) \,(height) = \frac{1}{2} \,a \,b\sin C \) but from right angled \(\triangle BCD \) we have \(\sin D = \frac{BC}{BD} = \frac{a}{2r}\)
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \sin D = \sin A = \frac{a}{2r} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, \frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{1}{2r}. \end{equation*}

    Subsection 3.6.2 Approximatation and error analysis

    Remember
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{h\to 0} f(a)= \frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h} =f'(a) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, f(a+h)= f(a)+ hf'(a) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Errors
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\nabla y}{\nabla x} \approx. \frac{dy}{dx} \, \Rightarrow \, \nabla y = \nabla x \frac{dy}{dx} \end{equation*}
    The term \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) is derivative at certain point. Also,
    \begin{equation*} \text{ Relative Error } = \frac{\nabla y}{y} \end{equation*}
    and \(\text{ Change } = \nabla = y_2-y_1\)
    🔗

    Example 3.6.12.

    Find the approximate value of \(\sqrt{36.6}.\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} Let, \, f(x) \amp =\sqrt{x} \\ or, f'(x) \amp =\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x} } \end{align*}
    \begin{align*} again, \, a+h \amp = 36.6 = 36 + 0.6 \\ \therefore \, a \amp =36 \, \text{ and } \, h=0.6 \end{align*}
    here \(a=36\) is the closest value of 36.6 whose square root is known. hence,
    \begin{align*} f'(a) \amp = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{36} } =\frac{1}{12}\\ but, \, f(a+h) \amp = f(a)+hf'(a)\\ \therefore \sqrt{36.6} \amp = 6 + 0.6\times \frac{1}{12} = 6.05. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.13.

    Find approximate value of \(f(x) = x^2-2x-3\) at \(x=1.999.\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} f(x) = x^2-2x-3 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} f'(x) = 2x-2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} also, \, a+h = 1.999 \end{equation*}
    where \(a=2\) closest point and \(h=-0.001,\) and
    \begin{equation*} f'(a) = 2\times 2-2=2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} but, \, f(a+h) = f(a) +hf'(a) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} but, \, 1.999 = (4-4-3) -0.001\times 2 = -3-0.002 =-3.002 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.14.

    Find approximate value of \((33)^{\frac{1}{5}}.\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} f(x) = x^{\frac{1}{5}} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} f'(x) = \frac{1}{5}x^{\frac{-4}{5}} = \frac{1}{5x^{\frac{-4}{5}}} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} now, \, f(a+h) = f(a) +hf'(a) \end{equation*}
    also \(33=32+1 = 2^5 +1 = a+h\)
    \begin{equation*} f'(x)\vert_{x=a} = \frac{1}{5\cdot 2^{5\frac{-4}{5}}} = \frac{1}{5\times 2^4}=\frac{1}{80} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 33^{1/5} = 32^{1/5} + 1\times \frac{1}{80} = 2+\frac{1}{80}= \frac{161}{80} =2.0125. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.15.

    Approximate value of \(\cos 31^o\) is ....
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} Let \, f(x) = \cos x \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, f'(x) = -\sin x \end{equation*}
    Now convert \(31^o \) into real number, i.e., \(31^o = 30^o+1^o = \frac{\pi}{6}+ \frac{\pi}{180}=a+h\) hence, \(a=\frac{\pi}{6} \) and \(h= \frac{\pi}{180}.\)
    \begin{equation*} Now, \, f'(a) = -\sin (\frac{\pi}{6}) = -\frac{1}{2}. \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, f(a+h) = f(a) +hf'(a) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, \cos 31^o = \cos \frac{\pi}{6} +\frac{\pi}{180}\times -\frac{1}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{\pi}{360} =0.857. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.16.

    If radius of sphere is measured \(7\, m\) with an error of \(0.02 \, m\text{,}\) then find approximate error in its volume.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(r= 7m, \, \nabla r = 0.02 \, m,\) \(\nabla V = ?\) we have
    \begin{equation*} V= \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, \frac{dV}{dr} = 4\pi r^2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} But, \, \nabla V = \nabla r \frac{dV}{dr} = 0.02\times 4\pi 7^2 = 12.31 \, m^3. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.17.

    Find the approximate change in the volume \(V\) of a cube of side \(x\) meters caused by increaseing the side by 2%.
    🔗
    Solution.
    For a cube
    \begin{align*} V \amp =x^3\\ or, \, \frac{dV}{dx} \amp =3x^2\\ also, \, \frac{\nabla x}{x} \amp = 2\%\\ or, \, \frac{\nabla x}{x} \amp = \frac{2}{100} = 0.02\\ or, \, \nabla x \amp = 0.02x \end{align*}
    \begin{equation*} Now, \, \nabla V = \nabla x \frac{dV}{dx} = 0.02x \times 3x^2 = 0.06x^3. \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, \frac{\nabla V}{V} =\frac{0.06x^3}{x^3} =0.06 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} Hence, \, \%\frac{\nabla V}{V} =\frac{0.06x^3}{x^3} =6\%. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    alternately, from physics if \(V=x^3\)
    \begin{equation*} \nabla V=3\nabla x = 3\times 2\% = 6\%. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.18.

    Find approximate valume of metal in a hollow spherical shell whose internal and external radii are 3 and 3.0005 cm respectively.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} V=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dV}{dr} =4\pi r^2 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, \nabla V = \nabla r \frac{dV}{dr} = 0.0005\times (4\pi\times 3^2) = 0.018\pi \, cm^3. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.19.

    In \(\triangle ABC\) if side length \(a, \, b, \) remain constant such that \(\alpha \) is the eroor in \(\angle C\) then find relative error in its area.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given: \(\nabla C = \alpha \) then \(\nabla S =?\text{.}\) From sine formula of a triangle
    \begin{equation*} Area \, S = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} or, \, \frac{dS}{dC} = \frac{1}{2}ab\cos C \end{equation*}
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} Now, \,\nabla S = \nabla C \frac{dS}{dC} = \alpha\frac{dS}{dC} = \alpha\frac{1}{2}ab\cos C \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \therefore \, \frac{\nabla S}{S} =\frac{\alpha\frac{1}{2}ab\cos C}{\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C} =\alpha \cot C. \end{equation*}

    Subsection 3.6.3 Tangent and Normal at a Point

    Equation of tangent at a point (x_1,y_1) with slope of \(m\) is given as
    \begin{equation*} y-y_1 = m(x-x_1) \end{equation*}
    and an equation of normal at tangent at the given point is given as
    \begin{equation*} y-y_1 =-\frac{1}{m}(x-x_1) \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Proof.

    The slope of a tangent line through a given point \(P(x_1,y_1)\) is given by
    \begin{equation*} slope=m_t=\tan \theta = \frac{rise}{run} =\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation} or, \, y-y_1 = m_t(x-x_1)\tag{3.6.4} \end{equation}
    by taking \(x_2\) as \(x\) and \(y_2\) as \(y.\)
    🔗
    Now from figure, we have
    \begin{equation*} m_1 =\tan\theta \end{equation*}
    and the slope for normal line
    \begin{align*} m_n=\tan\phi \amp = \tan(180^o-(90^o-\theta)) \\ \amp = \tan(90^o+\theta) = -\cot \theta\\ \therefore \, m_n \amp = - \frac{1}{\tan\theta} = - \frac{1}{m_t} \end{align*}
    Hence, the equation of normal line through \(P(x_1, y_1)\) is given as
    \begin{equation} y-y_1 = m_n(x-x_1) = - \frac{1}{m_t}(x-x_1)\tag{3.6.5} \end{equation}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.6.20.

    Find equation of tangent and normal to \(y=\frac{4}{x^2+2}\) at \(x=0.\)
    🔗
    Solution.

    If a function \(f(x)\) is defined in an interval \([a,b]\) such that:
    1. \(f(x) \) is continuous in \([a,b]\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. \(f(x) \) is also differentiable in \((a,b)\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. and \(f(a) =f(b)\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    then there must exist at least one point \(c\) such that \(c \in (a,b)\) where
    \begin{equation*} f'(c) =0 \text{.} \end{equation*}
    Or, in other words \(f(x)\) has a critical point in \((a,b)\text{.}\) Here, slope of function at point/s \(c\) is parallel to the slope of horizontal secant line through \(AB.\)

    Definition 3.6.29.

    • A function \(y=f(x)\) is said to have local maxima (or, local extrema) at \(x=a\) if value of function at that point is greater than or equal to values of function in some neighbourhood of \(x=a.\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • A function \(y=f(x)\) is said to have local minima (or, local extrema) at \(x=a\) if value of function at that point is less than or equal to values of function in some neighbourhood of \(x=a.\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • A function \(y=f(x)\) is said to have global maxima (or, absolute maxima) at \(x=a\) if \(f(a) \geq f(x) \, \forall_x\) in domain of \(f(x).\)

      here, \(10x-10 =0\) gives \(f'(x) =0\) but \(x-2 =0 \, \Rightarrow \, x=2\) makes \(f'(x) \) undefined.
      \begin{equation*} hence, \, 10x-10 =0 \, \Rightarrow \, x=1 \end{equation*}
      Thus the critical points are \(1,2.\)
