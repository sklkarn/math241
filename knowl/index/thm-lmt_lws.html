Theorem 2.2.1.

If \(c\) is a constant and the limits \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x)\) and \(\lim_{x \to a} g(x)\) exist as finite numbers not as \(-\infty, \) or \(\infty\text{,}\) then
  1. \begin{equation*} \lim_{x \to a} \left[f(x) \pm g(x)\right] = \lim_{x \to a} f(x) \pm \lim_{x \to a} g(x) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \begin{equation*} \lim_{x \to a} \left[c f(x)\right] = c \lim_{x \to a} f(x) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \begin{equation*} \lim_{x \to a} \left[f(x)\times g(x)\right] = \lim_{x \to a} f(x) \times \lim_{x \to a} g(x) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  4. \begin{equation*} \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{\lim_{x \to a} f(x)}{ \lim_{x \to a} g(x)}; \text{ provided } \lim_{x \to a} g(x)\neq 0 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  5. \begin{equation*} \lim_{x \to a}\left( f(x)\right)^{g(x)} =\left(\lim_{x \to a}f(x) \right)^{\lim_{x \to a}g(x)} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  6. If \(f(x) \lt g(x) \) then \(\lim_{x\to a} f(x) \leq \lim_{x\to a} g(x)\)
    🔗
    e.g., if \(0\lt x\lt 1 \) then \(x^2 \lt x\) but \(x \to 0 \) then \(x^2 = x\text{.}\)

    🔗
